Why Do the Same Problems Keep Coming Back?

You notice yourself putting off something important again. You spend too much time thinking about what happened, second-guess a decision you already made, or react to a familiar situation in a way you wish you hadn't. You know what you want to change, but you still don't understand why it keeps happening.

Perhaps you're only beginning to look for a way forward. Or perhaps you've already tried a great deal: reading books, doing exercises, changing habits, and learning to manage your thoughts and emotions. Some of these approaches helped. At times, you may even have felt that the problem was finally behind you. But eventually, the familiar reaction returned.

Imagine putting out a campfire while a small ember continues to glow beneath the ashes. It's almost invisible, yet under the right conditions, that ember can start another fire. Something similar can sometimes happen with our recurring problems.

We work to change our behavior, overcome inner resistance, and deal with troubling thoughts. Sometimes we succeed. Then a familiar situation arises, an old emotional state returns, and we find ourselves right back where we thought we'd moved beyond. Perhaps we've learned how to put out the flames, but haven't yet reached the glowing ember underneath.

That idea is at the heart of the Shadrin Method, an original practical approach to self-help. It invites us to work with the emotional charge experienced when a habitual reaction first takes shape. The goal is to reduce the influence of recurring emotional reactions on behavior, creating more freedom to act according to our own decisions instead of being driven by familiar inner resistance.
