Overthinking: What Happens Inside

Articles›Overthinking

Sometimes thoughts return to the same situation, even after we have thought it through. Behind this familiar mental loop may be different inner states: anxiety, shame, resentment, fear of making a mistake, or painful uncertainty.

This is where the work begins—with understanding your own inner state. Here you can identify the experience that feels familiar, understand how it relates to recurring thoughts, and explore a practical approach to working through that inner reaction and breaking out of the cycle.

What stays with you?

Choose the situation that feels familiar. Recognizing what you're experiencing is a first step toward working with it.

Article 01 · In preparation

When a work conversation keeps replaying

The meeting ended an hour ago. Your next task is open on the screen, but the exchange with a colleague or manager is still present. You may notice tightness, resentment, or the feeling that you never quite finished saying what mattered.

The question is not only what was said. It is also what remains inside after the conversation: a reaction that may continue even when there is nothing useful left to add to the discussion. A coming article will explore this distinction through meetings, briefings and professional exchanges.

Tension after communication · Unfinishedness · Resentment · Emotional unfinishedness

Article 02 · In preparation

When a decision is made but doubt stays

You reviewed the options, discussed the risks and chose a direction. Yet the choice still feels exposed. A small possibility of error can start another round of checking, even when no new facts have appeared.

This is where overthinking may be less about calculating a better answer than about the discomfort of being responsible for a decision. Our next piece in this direction will look at fear of mistakes, self-doubt and anticipated regret in working life.

Fear of mistakes · Self-doubt · Fear of regret

Article 03 · In preparation

When feedback stays with you

A supervisor questions part of your work. You address the issue, perhaps even agree with the feedback, but the moment still carries weight. The feeling may be shame, anger, vulnerability or an uneasy sense of being watched.

Sometimes the feedback was unfair. Sometimes it was reasonable but unexpectedly painful. Those are different situations. The forthcoming article will examine the inner experience of professional evaluation without assuming that every difficult reaction has the same cause.

Shame · Sensitivity to judgment · Internal sense of threat

Article 04 · In preparation

When the next meeting has already started in your mind

Tomorrow's review is still hours away, but your mind is already staging the questions and possible outcomes. The anticipation can have its own physical and emotional weight long before anything has happened.

Preparation can be valuable. Rehearsing the same feared possibility without new information may feel very different. A forthcoming article will explore anticipatory anxiety, uncertainty and the tension of waiting for an outcome.

Anxiety · Painful uncertainty · Anticipatory anxiety
